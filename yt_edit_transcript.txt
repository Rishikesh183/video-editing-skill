**Editing pipeline to reproduce**

1. **Inspect the footage.** Transcribe audio and extract frames at regular intervals. Combine spoken content with visual observations to understand the recording.
2. **Plan assets.** Decide where cuts, motion graphics, screenshots, existing B-roll, or generated visuals would help.
3. **Create the rough cut.** Remove silence, dead air, mistakes, and unusable takes.
4. **Audit the cut.** Check that edits preserve meaning and continuity.
5. **Map frame geometry.** Locate faces, hands, and important objects. Use their coordinates to keep text and graphics from covering them.
6. **Select or create assets.** Capture relevant screens, search the existing B-roll library, and generate visuals when needed. The creator describes indexing roughly 500 B-roll clips in an `index.md` file containing descriptions of their contents.
7. **Apply the chosen style.** Add layouts, typography, motion graphics, captions, and sound.
8. **Render and review.** Inspect the result for misplaced elements and other defects; revise before delivery.

**How to learn an editing style from a reference**

- Inspect a reference video using frame sampling and transcription. The demonstration samples **two frames per second**.
- Extract explicit rules for framing, layout changes, typography, colors, animations, and pacing.
- Record how much time the reference spends on full-screen presenter footage, split-screen layouts, and visuals without the presenter.
- Save the findings as a style document or reusable skill.
- Apply those rules to new footage.
- **Produce preview frames or a contact sheet before the full render.** The creator explicitly requests this during the demonstration.
- Refine the output and save useful corrections into the reusable instructions.

**Main lesson for an AI agent:** Separate general production skills from style-specific instructions. Reuse the same inspection, cutting, asset, geometry, and review stages across different editing styles.
